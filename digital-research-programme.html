Trestle Digital OSINT Research Programme

Social Media Analysis, Digital Research Skills, and Narrative Mapping

Trestle Consulting runs an ongoing Digital OSINT Research Programme designed to help people build practical social media research and open-source intelligence (OSINT) skills through structured, mentored research work.

This is not a formal internship and not an employment scheme. It is a voluntary, unpaid research programme intended to help participants develop real, demonstrable digital research capability, particularly in areas where access to formal internships or elite pathways is limited.

The programme focuses on public social media analysis, online narrative tracking, and digital ethnography — the kinds of skills increasingly expected in journalism, research, communications, policy, NGOs, think tanks, and OSINT-adjacent roles.

What this programme is for

The purpose of the programme is to augment participants’ digital research capability by giving them:

  • Hands-on experience with real OSINT-style research tasks.
  • Structured research prompts rather than vague “go research this” requests.
  • Guidance and oversight from experienced OSINT practitioners and research professionals.
  • A chance to build evidence of competence, not just interest.
  • A credible reference point for future job or study applications.
  • A pathway to publish research publicly as part of a growing portfolio.

This programme exists because many capable people are excluded from formal research and intelligence pipelines, not because they lack ability, but because they lack access.

What we focus on

The research work centres on publicly visible online material, including:

  • Social media platforms (X, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, Telegram, Reddit, YouTube).
  • Public groups, channels, pages, and comment threads.
  • Viral or semi-viral narratives.
  • Recurring themes, symbols, and framing strategies.
  • How stories, claims, and interpretations spread across platforms.

The emphasis is on how narratives form and circulate, not on identifying individuals or monitoring private spaces.

What participants actually do

Participants contribute by carrying out structured OSINT-style research tasks, such as:

Locate and map content

Find publicly available posts, videos, or images related to a topic and map where a narrative appears across platforms.

Capture framing details

Record captions, hashtags, and framing language while documenting how comment sections interpret or reinforce claims.

Log and compare findings

Log examples using shared templates (links + short notes) and compare how the same content is reframed in different online spaces.

This is documentary and analytical groundwork, not speculative interpretation.

Participants are not expected to:

  • Scrape data.
  • Access private or closed groups.
  • Engage with communities under study.
  • Make judgments about intent or belief.
  • Write intelligence reports or policy briefs.

All synthesis and final writing is overseen by Trestle.

Mentorship and oversight

Research tasks are:

  • Designed and reviewed by people with OSINT and research backgrounds.
  • Structured to teach how to look, not what to think.
  • Scoped so participants are not exposed to unsafe or disturbing material.
  • Reviewed for quality, accuracy, and methodological discipline.

The aim is to help participants understand:

  • How professional social media research is conducted.
  • How to document findings clearly.
  • How to avoid common analytical mistakes.
  • How to talk about research work credibly in applications or interviews.

Publishing and portfolios

As the programme develops, Trestle will host a public research and writing space on its website.

Participants who wish to do so may:

  • Publish short research notes or essays.
  • Collaborate on longer pieces.
  • Link to their work as part of a personal portfolio.

Publishing is optional and subject to review. The goal is to give people something concrete to point to when applying for jobs, freelance work, further study, or research roles.

Who this programme is for

This programme is especially intended for people who:

  • Are interested in OSINT, social media analysis, or digital research.
  • Did not go through elite university pipelines.
  • Are not coming from the military, intelligence services, or civil service.
  • Are self-taught, working-class, career-changing, or outside formal networks.
  • May be older (30s, 40s, 50s+) and looking to formalise existing skills.
  • Are students who want experience that goes beyond coursework.
  • Enjoy internet culture, narrative analysis, and careful observation.
  • Want to build a research track record, not just a CV line.

Formal qualifications are not required. Curiosity, reliability, and care matter more than credentials.

What this is not

This programme is not:

  • An employment offer.
  • A guaranteed route to paid work.
  • A surveillance or monitoring operation.
  • A covert intelligence project.
  • A political campaigning initiative.
  • A way to target or profile individuals or groups.

It is a skills-building research environment focused on publicly visible online culture.

Ethics, safety, and boundaries

All work operates within clear limits:

  • Public material only.
  • No deception or impersonation.
  • No engagement with subjects.
  • No private data collection.
  • No doxxing or identification work.
  • No enforcement or intervention activity.

Participants can refuse tasks, set boundaries, and withdraw at any time.

Programme documents

Before contributing, participants will be asked to read and agree to the following documents:

  • Programme Charter — what the programme is, its purpose, and its limits.
  • Terms of Participation — voluntary, unpaid participation and expectations.
  • Contributor Agreement — how contributions may be used and credited.
  • Research Ethics Policy — ethical boundaries and safeguards.
  • Methodology Statement — how research is conducted and interpreted.
  • Data Handling & Privacy Statement — what data is collected and what is excluded.
